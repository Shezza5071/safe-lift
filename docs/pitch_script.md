# Pitch script: the problem (under 1 minute)

For the opening of the pitch, before or over the motion graphic (`demo/safe_lift_motion.mp4`, slide 2 of `demo/safe_lift_pitch.pptx`). About 150 words each.

## Version 2: story-led (use this one)

> It's 3 am in an Adelaide aged care home. The resident's plan says two staff and a hoist. But the second carer is at the other end of the corridor, and the resident needs to move now. So one carer bends, twists and lifts alone.
>
> That moment happens thousands of times a night across South Australia, where 17,358 people live in residential aged care.
>
> It's why body stressing makes up nearly half of all serious injury claims in Australia's care workforce. It's why, in South Australia, health care and social assistance has more body-stressing claims than any other industry.
>
> More training won't fix it. On its own, training doesn't prevent back injury. What works is following the plan: the right equipment and enough hands, every time.
>
> Safe Lift is built for that moment. It checks the plan before anyone lifts, and coaches the lift while it happens.

**Delivery**
- Slow down on the opening scene and let "lifts alone" land before the numbers.
- The 3 am carer is an illustrative scenario, not a real case. Keep it in present tense; don't present it as "a carer we spoke to" (no trainer quote was obtained; see `docs/evidence.md`).
- "Thousands of times a night" is a rhetorical estimate. If a judge might push on it, say **"That moment happens every shift"** instead.

## Version 1: numbers-led (backup)

> Seventeen thousand, three hundred and fifty-eight South Australians live in residential aged care. Every time one of them moves from bed to chair, a care worker is doing a hazardous manual task.
>
> And it's hurting them. Body stressing is nearly half of all serious injury claims in Australia's care workforce: 47.6 per cent. In more than half of those claims, the cause is the person being moved. In South Australia, health care and social assistance has more body-stressing claims than any other industry.
>
> We already know what *doesn't* fix this. Training on its own doesn't prevent back injury. What protects workers is the right equipment, enough staff, and following the resident's handling plan.
>
> But in a busy shift, the plan says two staff and a hoist, and one worker lifts alone.
>
> That's the gap Safe Lift closes: plan first, then lift.

**Delivery**
- Pause after "17,358" and after "one worker lifts alone"; the second pause sets up the video or demo.
- Over the video, start at the first frame: the on-screen numbers line up with the first 25 seconds.

## Sources (both versions)

- **17,358 in SA residential aged care (30 June 2025):** based on AIHW, GEN Aged Care Data, People using aged care by region 2024-25, CC BY 4.0, via H4H.
- **47.6% body stressing; 54.5% caused by the person being moved:** Safe Work Australia, *WHS profile: nursing, care and support workforce* (May 2025).
- **SA: health care and social assistance has the most body-stressing claims (21%):** SafeWork SA, *Campaign to combat body stressing claims*, 21 October 2024. Say "health care and social assistance", not "health care workers".
- **Training alone doesn't prevent back injury:** Cochrane review CD005958.pub3.
- **Equipment, staffing and the plan as the controls:** Safe Work Australia, *Model Code of Practice: Hazardous manual tasks* (hierarchy of controls).

Full citations and what each source does and doesn't support: `docs/evidence.md`.
